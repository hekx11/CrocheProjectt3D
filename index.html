    Writing your own pattern

    Write it the way patterns are usually written. These few habits help the 3D view get it right.

    1. Give each part a name

    Put the name on a line of its own, before its rounds. Add (make 2) for pairs.

    BODY
ARMS (make 2)

    2. One round or row per line

    Start with the round number and end with the stitch count in brackets. The count is checked for you.

    Rnd 3: (sc, inc) x6 (18)
Rnds 5-12: sc around (24)

    3. Say how each part is finished

    Stuff the body firmly.
Do not stuff. Flatten the ears.
Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing.

    4. Sewing parts together

    Write one sentence, right after the part you are sewing on. It needs four things:

    Sew the arms to the sides of the body between Rnds 9 and 11.

    • Which part Use the name from its heading (arms).
    • Sewn to what The name of the other part (body).
    • Which side Use one of: top, sides, front, back, bottom.
    • Which rounds “between Rnds 9 and 11” or “at Rnd 10”. Count the rounds on the part you are sewing onto, here the body.

    For a pair, you can also say how far apart they sit: add about 8 sts apart.

    Sew the ears to the top of the head between Rnds 3 and 6, about 8 sts apart.
Sew the muzzle to the front of the head between Rnds 8 and 11.
Sew the tail to the back of the body at Rnd 6.

    If you leave out the side or the rounds, the part is placed at a best guess and a blue dot in Pattern check tells you so. The same works for eyes: “Insert safety eyes between Rnds 6 and 7, 5 sts apart.”
